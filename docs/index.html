The getting-started guide

Two speakers.
One listening space.

Speakerr turns two separate Mac audio outputs into a synchronized stereo pair. Follow this guide to route your system audio, measure speaker timing, and start playback with confidence.

Start the tutorial ↓ Download for macOS ↗

Quick start · about 5 minutes

From download to playback.

There are four things to do before you press play. The app keeps the order visible, so you always know what comes next.

01

Install the audio bridge

Install BlackHole 2ch, the free virtual input that lets Speakerr receive audio from every Mac app.

02

Choose your outputs

Open Speakerr from the menu bar and select exactly two connected speakers.

03

Calibrate in position

Place your Mac where you listen. Speakerr measures each speaker and compensates for the timing difference.

04

Start the session

Choose the synchronized group as your output, start playback, and enjoy the same signal from both speakers.

●Before you begin: use macOS 15 or later on Apple Silicon. Have both speakers connected, and keep the Mac near your normal listening position.

How it works

A clean signal path.

Speakerr keeps system audio and calibration separate: the microphone is used briefly to measure timing, not to record ordinary playback.

Step 01 · installation

Install the pieces.

BlackHole is required for programme audio. Speakerr uses it as the input, then sends processed audio to your real outputs.

01 / BlackHole

Install the virtual audio driver

  1. Install with Homebrew:
  2. brew install blackhole-2ch
  3. Or use the BlackHole release installer ↗.
02 / Speakerr

Install the app

  1. Download the latest DMG from GitHub Releases ↗.
  2. Open the DMG and drag Speakerr to Applications.
  3. If macOS asks, choose Open from the right-click menu.
03 / macOS audio

Route your system audio

  1. Open System Settings → Sound → Output.
  2. Select BlackHole 2ch.
  3. Keep this setting while Speakerr is playing. Your normal apps will now feed the virtual input.
04 / permission

Allow microphone access

macOS classifies audio-input access as microphone access. Select Allow when prompted. Speakerr needs it for the calibration measurement only.

↗Denied it earlier? Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and enable Speakerr.

Steps 02–03 · setup

Select, then align.

The main window is organized around one reliable flow: Speakers → Calibration → Playback.

Speakers

Select exactly two outputs

  1. Click Choose Speakers… on first launch (or Change Speakers… later).
  2. Check two connected output devices, such as two USB speakers or a built-in speaker and an interface.
  3. Click Use Selected. Choose Stereo-preserving or Mono broadcast in Playback mode.
Calibration

Measure from your listening position

  1. Put the Mac at ear height where you normally sit or stand.
  2. Click Calibrate…, choose the microphone, and set a comfortable calibration volume.
  3. Click Start Calibration. Stay still and quiet while Speakerr measures both speakers.
  4. Wait for Speakers Aligned. The residual error shows the remaining timing difference.

“Keep the Mac near your normal listening position.”

Calibration rule of thumb

Step 04 · everyday use

Play, refine, repeat.

Once your speakers are aligned, the menu-bar app keeps the session ready while you work in any audio application.

Playback

Start and pause Speakerr

In the Playback section, click Resume Speakerr. The status changes to Running when programme audio is attached. Use Pause Speakerr to stop processing without losing your speaker selection.

Routing modes

Choose the right presentation

Use Stereo-preserving when left/right imaging matters. Use Mono broadcast when both speakers should play the same content, such as announcements or a multi-room setup.

Recheck

Keep alignment healthy

Reconnects, sample-rate changes, and sleep can make calibration stale. Click Recheck after a change. If Speakerr reports drift, use Apply Correction or calibrate again.

Advanced

Inspect timing details

Expand Advanced to see manual, calibration, and dynamic delay for each speaker. Open Diagnostics… when troubleshooting device or stream issues.

If something sounds wrong

A short troubleshooting list.

Most setup problems come from one of three connections: the virtual input, the selected outputs, or microphone permission.

No sound from either speaker

Confirm macOS Output is BlackHole 2ch, Speakerr shows two connected speakers, and Playback says Running. Then pause and resume the session.

Only one speaker plays

Open Change Speakers… and confirm both devices are still connected. USB, Bluetooth, and HDMI devices may disappear after sleep.

Calibration cannot measure

Move the Mac closer to the listening position, reduce background noise, and try again with a slightly higher calibration volume. Check microphone permission.

Audio drifts after reconnecting

Speakerr marks calibration stale after route changes. Click Recheck; if needed, run the full calibration again from your normal listening position.
